Use this page when an [app connection](/compute/networking/private-networking#connect-to-another-app) or private DNS doesn't work as expected. The examples use a `web` app with an app connection named `payments`.

Start with your running deployment, not the **Connections** page. The page shows defaults for future deployments. A running deployment uses the connections saved when Unkey created it. Read the hostname from the running app's variable, and check whether the deployment is older than your last connection change.

## The host variable is missing

Symptom: `PAYMENTS_HOST` is empty in your app.

Cause: Unkey adds connection variables when it creates a deployment. The running deployment is older than the connection, or the connection belongs to the other environment.

Solution:

1. On the **Connections** page, check that the environment selector shows the environment of your deployment.
2. Select the connection and confirm its variable name.
3. Redeploy your app in that environment.

## The hostname doesn't exist

Symptom: DNS lookups fail with a "not found" error, such as `NXDOMAIN` or `ENOTFOUND`.

Cause: The calling deployment has no connection with that name, or no private DNS. Common reasons:

- The connection was added or renamed after the deployment was created.
- The target app was deleted.
- The code uses a short name such as `payments` instead of `payments.unkey.internal`.
- The deployment belongs to another app or environment than the connection.
- The deployment has no private DNS. In that case `UNKEY_PRIVATE_DOMAIN` is also missing. See [UNKEY_PRIVATE_DOMAIN is missing](#unkey_private_domain-is-missing).

Solution: Read the hostname from the connection variable instead of hard-coding it. Redeploy your app after you add or rename a connection.

## The hostname fails to resolve

Symptom: DNS lookups fail with a server error, such as `SERVFAIL` or `EAI_AGAIN`.

Cause: The connection exists, but its target rule selects no running deployment with ready instances. Common reasons:

- The target app has no live production deployment.
- A **Same Git branch** target has no preview from your deployment's branch.
- The selected preview stopped after it went idle, or someone stopped it.
- Your app deploys from a container image and uses **Same Git branch**.
- A region prefix such as `eu-west-1.` names a region without ready instances.

Solution: Check the target rule that your deployment saved. Deploy or wake the target, or [choose another target](/compute/networking/private-networking#choose-which-deployment-you-reach) and redeploy your app. Private traffic can't wake a stopped preview.

## The connection is refused or times out

Symptom: The hostname resolves, but connections fail.

Cause: DNS works, but the target doesn't accept the traffic. Common reasons:

- The target app listens on `localhost` instead of `0.0.0.0`.
- Your app connects to the wrong port.
- The client uses IPv6. Private hostnames resolve to IPv4 addresses only.
- The test uses `ping`. ICMP traffic is denied.
- The target calls back without its own connection. Connections are one-way.
- A long-lived connection still points at a deployment that was replaced more than 30 minutes ago.

Solution: Make the target listen on `0.0.0.0` and the port your client uses. Test with a TCP or HTTP request instead of `ping`. For traffic in the other direction, add a connection on the target app. Reconnect clients after the target changes.

## The hostname reaches the wrong deployment

Symptom: Requests reach an older or unexpected version of the target app.

Cause: The target rule selects a different deployment than you expect, or the client reuses an old connection. Common reasons:

- The connection is pinned to a specific deployment.
- The default changed after the calling deployment was created.
- The target app is rolled back, so **production** follows the rolled-back deployment.
- The newest preview hasn't reached ready yet, so the previous one still answers.
- The client reuses a connection that it opened before the switch.

Solution: Redeploy your app to use changed defaults. Promote a target deployment to end its rollback. Resolve the hostname again and open a new connection.

## New deployments of your app fail

Symptom: Creating a deployment of your app fails after you stopped a target deployment.

Cause: A connection default pins a deployment that isn't running anymore. Unkey can't save a pin to a stopped deployment.

Solution: [Choose another target](/compute/networking/private-networking#choose-which-deployment-you-reach) for the connection, or wake the pinned deployment. Then deploy your app again.

## UNKEY_PRIVATE_DOMAIN is missing

Symptom: `UNKEY_PRIVATE_DOMAIN` is empty.

Cause: The deployment has no private DNS. Common reasons:

- The deployment was created before private networking was available for it.
- The app slug isn't a valid DNS label, for example because it's longer than 63 characters.
- The region doesn't have private DNS.

Solution: Redeploy the app. You don't need a connection. If the variable is still missing, contact support. See [Reach instances of the same deployment](/compute/networking/private-networking#reach-instances-of-the-same-deployment).

## A connection can't be added or saved

Symptom: The dashboard shows an error when you add, rename, or retarget a connection.

Cause: The change breaks a connection rule:

- Your app already has a connection to that app in this environment.
- The name is already used in this environment, or it matches your app's slug.
- The name starts with `unkey` or has invalid characters.
- The selected deployment isn't ready and running.

Solution: Edit the existing connection instead of adding a second one, or choose another name or target. See [Hostnames and variables](/compute/networking/private-networking#hostnames-and-variables).
