---
title: Fair use guidelines
description: "Non-binding operational guidance for running Compute workloads on shared infrastructure."
---

These fair use guidelines provide non-binding operational guidance for Unkey
Compute. They help customers run legitimate development and production
workloads without threatening the security or reliability of shared
infrastructure.

Plan quotas and metering remain the technical controls for resource allocation
and usage. High usage by itself isn't considered misuse when it comes from a
legitimate workload that operates within those controls.

## Appropriate usage

Common Compute usage includes:

- Hosting APIs and applications for your organization or customers
- Running production, preview, and development deployments
- Autoscaling in response to legitimate user traffic
- Running background work that supports your application
- Conducting authorized tests coordinated with Unkey when they could create a
  sustained traffic spike

## Patterns that are never fair use

The following patterns are never fair use:

- Circumventing quotas, metering, billing, rate limits, security controls, or
  enforcement through additional accounts, workspaces, projects, deployments,
  or other means
- Running unauthorized load tests or denial-of-service tests
- Mining cryptocurrency
- Operating open proxies or unrelated traffic relays
- Running or distributing malware
- Sending spam

These examples don't limit Unkey's ability to address other workloads that
threaten the platform or its customers.

## Operational response

Unkey may contact the workspace owner to understand unusual usage and request
changes to the workload. Unkey may restrict workloads that threaten platform
security or reliability, including immediately when necessary to protect the
platform or other customers.

Before an authorized load test or an unusually large launch, email
[support@unkey.com](mailto:support@unkey.com). Include the workspace ID,
expected CPU, memory, and disk requirements, regions, replica count, traffic
volume, and test duration.

Review [Limits](/limits) for plan quotas, allocation calculations, and boundary
behavior.
